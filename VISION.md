# Business Software, Reimagined

## Slogan

**Start with the business, not the software.**

**You describe your business. The software appears.**

Poseidon exists to make software form itself around a business. People should be able to explain what they do, see a functioning system take shape, and change it as naturally as they describe a change in their work.

## A different starting point

Today, businesses choose software, configure it, adapt their processes to its assumptions, buy plugins, and hire consultants to fill the gaps. Understanding the business is only the beginning; translating it into software becomes a project of its own.

Poseidon should make that process feel like it belongs to another generation:

**Business → describe how it works → working bespoke software.**

The business is the starting point. Its people, language, relationships, and ways of working should shape the system.

## The category jump

Breakthrough products change what people expect from a category. The iPhone was more than a better BlackBerry, the iPod more than a better MP3 player, and the iMac more than a prettier PC. The experience made the old comparison feel inadequate.

Poseidon's ambition is that same shift in business software. Competing on features, configuration options, or AI capabilities would leave it in the familiar contest between ERPs and low-code platforms. The stronger promise is software that already fits the company because it was formed around the company.

The moment that matters is when someone says: **“This is my business, working.”**

## The first sixty seconds

Someone opens an empty Poseidon and says:

> We're a dental clinic. We have four dentists, patients book appointments, dentists have different schedules, we take deposits and need reminders.

The target experience is that thirty seconds later, they are looking at their business software: patients, a calendar, booking flows, permissions, dashboards, and workflows that work together. They can use the system immediately.

Then they say:

> Actually, hygienists can also do check-ups.

The system adapts. The user stays focused on how the clinic works, and Poseidon handles what that means for the software.

This is the product standard we are building toward: a functioning system with real behaviour and persistent data, beyond a generated mockup or a convincing demo.

**Blank screen → describe company → working bespoke software.**

## Never make users learn Poseidon to build with Poseidon

Users already understand their business. That understanding should be enough to begin.

They should describe customer type, appointments, flows, deposits, and schedules in their own words. Poseidon should carry the burden of translating those concepts into a coherent application. When a business decision is missing, it should ask a business question the user can answer.

The same principle applies after the first minute. As the business changes, its software should evolve through the same direct conversation, preserving the work already done.

Entities, dynamic forms, workflow builders, and automation engines are implementation capabilities. Their purpose is to make this experience possible; the product promise remains the working business software they produce.

## The Consigliere

Poseidon does not stop being useful once the software exists.

As it learns the business, its people, customers, processes, rules, history, and data, it becomes something closer to a deeply tenured employee than a traditional software product.

The kind of person who has been there for years. The one who knows how things actually work, why decisions were made, where information lives, who does what, and what happened before.

You should be able to ask:

> How do I onboard a new customer?

> Where do we get our supplies from?

> Why do we do it this way?

> What happened the last time we changed this?

> Who normally handles this?

And Poseidon should know.

Not because someone manually built a chatbot FAQ, but because Poseidon understands the company's software, documentation, conversations, processes, history, and data as parts of the same business.

That knowledge should also make it capable of acting:

> Which customers are most likely to cancel next month?

> Move tomorrow's appointments around without creating conflicts.

> Why did revenue fall this week?

> We are hiring another employee next month. Prepare the system for them.

Poseidon should already understand the context behind those requests.

It becomes the employee with the company wiki in their head, the history of every important decision, access to the systems, and the ability to actually get things done.

The user did not merely receive software. They gained a right-hand operator with deep institutional knowledge of the business.

The software is the body.

The intelligence inside it is the consigliere.

That is where the deeper value of Poseidon emerges: not simply software built around the business, but an intelligence that knows the business, remembers the business, and works within it.

## The standard for every feature

Every feature should bring the user closer to describing their business and having software that fits it. Judge it by these questions:

- Does it shorten the path from a business need to a working result?
- Can the user express what they want in the language of their business?
- Does it produce behaviour they can actually use and trust?
- Can they change their mind without learning how Poseidon is built?
- Does it make the whole system feel more like their company?

A feature that adds configuration power while making users learn more of the platform needs to be reconsidered. Technical sophistication earns its place by making the user's experience simpler.

Poseidon's success is measured by how quickly people can recognise their business in the software, put it to work, and keep shaping it as their business evolves.
